ABSTRACT

Local structure.
Whole-body coordination.

Translating language into humanoid motion requires coordinating body parts, determining motion duration, and producing references suitable for robot execution. PartiGen is a part-aware latent diffusion framework that incorporates anatomical structure into a shared whole-body motion representation. Part-specific attention processes anatomically masked frame features; the outputs are fused to combine local limb information with whole-body context.

Conditioned on text and motion history, PartiGen generates motion primitives autoregressively. Forward-kinematics supervision encourages kinematic consistency. After a complete candidate trajectory is generated, a learned end-of-sequence predictor selects a frame-level endpoint. A pretrained, frozen GRIT controller tracks the resulting references in MuJoCo and on the physical Unitree G1.

Part-aware feature processing

Fixed anatomical masks select joint-angle inputs before part-specific attention. Shared root and contact features retain global information; part outputs are fused into one whole-body representation.

Kinematic consistency

Forward-kinematics supervision and temporal smoothness regularize generated references during training. The generator and pretrained execution controller remain separate components.
